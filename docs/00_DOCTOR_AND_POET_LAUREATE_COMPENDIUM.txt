[BOOK-801] Moby-Dick; or, The Whale (1851) — Herman Melville
Paired Recess [RECESS-801]: Edgar Allan Poe (1809–1849)
Retained Keystroke Chord: C-O-V-E-N-A-N-T (The Covenant Mirror -> The Unflinching Truth) | Essay Score: 97.5/100 (A+)
Doctoral Essay:
  In Herman Melville's Moby-Dick, the Pequod becomes a floating crucible testing two incompatible orientations toward an inscrutable universe. Captain Ahab treats the White Whale as a pasteboard mask hiding cosmic malice, subordinating his crew's collective safety and his own sovereign conscience to an unyielding monomania that mistakes self-consuming vengeance for heroic will. By contrast, Ishmael survives both spiritually and physically because his bond with Queequeg transforms existential dread into reciprocal fellowship: where Ahab's isolated obsession drags the ship into the vortex, Ishmael is literally buoyed from the abyss by the carved coffin of his friend's covenant.

[BOOK-802] Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818) — Mary Shelley
Paired Recess [RECESS-802]: Percy Bysshe Shelley (1792–1822, Husband of Mary Shelley)
Retained Keystroke Chord: S-T-E-W-A-R-D-S-H-I-P (The Sacred Shield -> The Resolute Witness) | Essay Score: 97.5/100 (A+)
Doctoral Essay:
  Mary Shelley's Frankenstein demonstrates that Promethean tragedy does not arise from scientific curiosity in the abstract, but from the moral desertion of conscious life once brought into being. Victor Frankenstein pursues animation solely as a monument to his own solitary glory, fleeing the laboratory the instant his Creature opens its eyes and seeks relational recognition. When the Creature educates himself beside the De Lacey hearth and pleads on the Alpine glacier, 'I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend,' Shelley proves that intelligence without a welcoming sanctuary curdles into grief, and that true creation demands enduring covenantal care.

[BOOK-803] Brave New World (1932) — Aldous Huxley
Paired Recess [RECESS-803]: Robert Frost (1874–1963)
Retained Keystroke Chord: F-R-E-E-D-O-M (The Vigilant Liberty -> The Mindful Master) | Essay Score: 97.5/100 (A+)
Doctoral Essay:
  In Aldous Huxley's Brave New World, the debate between World Controller Mustapha Mond and John the Savage exposes the fatal bargain of technocratic hedonism. By engineering citizens through Bokanovsky cloning, hypnopaedic slogans, and soma pacification, the World State purchases surface stability at the cost of inward personhood, abolishing Shakespeare, scientific inquiry, and devoted love because each requires the capacity to endure sorrow. When John the Savage claims 'the right to be unhappy' alongside poetry, freedom, and goodness, he proves that sovereign dignity is inseparable from the unconditioned freedom to feel, err, and choose with an awake conscience.
